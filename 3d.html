Chapter 02 · 1993—2001

The camera became part of the controller.

Once games moved into three dimensions, the player had to steer a body and a camera at the same time. A small stick changed what a controller had to do.

Years
1993—2001
Exhibits
04
Focus
Movement → camera → analog control

1993

Doom

Doom put the player behind a first-person camera and made speed itself the appeal. Movement, aiming, and the shape of the room all happened in the same view.

Why it mattered

It taught players to read a 3D space through a camera, and it made the mouse and keyboard feel like a way to move through a world.

1994 / 1995

PlayStation

Sony's console brought 3D graphics and CD sound into the living room. It also made games feel like mainstream entertainment: a black box, a grey controller, and a shelf of discs.

Why it mattered

It turned 3D into a mass-market language, and made the disc the normal way to own a game.

1996

Nintendo 64

The Nintendo 64 put an analog stick in the middle of the controller. Instead of eight directions, the player could move through a continuous range of speeds and angles.

Why it mattered

It made 3D movement feel natural. Games could be designed around a camera, a character, and the space between them.

1997

DualShock

Two analog sticks and vibration turned the controller into a two-handed interface. One stick could move the character; the other could move the camera.

Why it mattered

It gave 3D games a standard control shape. The left stick moves, the right stick looks, and the buttons do everything else.
